Amazon Customer Review Analysis

Sep 2025 – Dec 2025

4-person team · I proposed the project, co-led data cleaning, and owned all ML/NLP work

Question
What drives customer ratings and product performance across Amazon categories, and what do unhappy customers actually complain about?
Data
102.5M reviews joined to 5.2M products across 10 categories, cleaned from raw files into Bronze and Silver Parquet layers with PySpark on Google Cloud Storage.
What I did
Ran LDA topic modeling on 17M+ one- and two-star reviews, extracting 30 topics that I grouped into 6 complaint themes (tech failures, durability, assembly, shipping and misleading listings, domain-specific issues, fit and sizing). Also analyzed how helpful votes and verified purchases relate to ratings.
Lesson
A Random Forest rating model explained little variance (R² 0.03), so I treated its feature importances as directional only and leaned on the EDA and NLP results.
Products whose critical reviews collected the most helpful votes averaged 2.7 stars, versus 4.0–4.5 for the rest, suggesting a potential early-warning signal for sellers.

Streaming · Data engineering

Epidemic Engine: Real-Time Data Pipeline & Alerting

Sep 2025 – Dec 2025

4-person team · I owned the TimescaleDB analytics store and the real-time alerting service

System
Simulated health events flow from Kafka through NiFi (routing and enrichment) back into Kafka, then Spark Structured Streaming aggregates them by region into TimescaleDB, all running on OpenShift (Kubernetes).
What I did
Deployed TimescaleDB and aligned its schema with the Spark output. Built a Python alerting service that checks three regional metrics every 20 minutes. When a threshold is crossed, it scores the value against a 7-day baseline with a z-score, assigns low, medium or high severity, suppresses repeats for 60 minutes per region and metric, and posts to Slack.
Why z-scores
A fixed threshold alone fires constantly in regions that are always busy and misses sudden spikes in quiet ones. Comparing each region with its own history, plus a cooldown, keeps alerts meaningful and avoids alert fatigue.

Boston Bluebikes Analysis

Sep 2024 – Dec 2024

3-person team · I worked across ingestion, processing and dashboards

Question
Where are Boston's bike-share stations out of balance, and how do events and traffic crashes relate to demand?
What I did
Built an Azure Medallion pipeline combining 12M+ trips (Nov 2021 – Nov 2024) with city event and crash data and live station status. Data Factory handled batch ingestion, Event Hub took the real-time station feed I collected from the public GBFS API, Databricks processed the Gold layer, and Synapse loaded Azure SQL for Power BI. Stations around downtown Boston showed the largest pickup/return imbalance, and events correlated with higher pickups, supporting dynamic rebalancing and coordination with event organizers.

Rivalry-Based Messaging Experiment

Jan 2026 – Mar 2026

5-person team

Question
Does a "beat the Yankees" appeal make Red Sox fans more willing to donate than a standard team-pride appeal?
What we did
Ran a randomized survey experiment in Qualtrics (n = 65; 31 control, 34 treatment) and confirmed the groups were balanced. Estimated the average treatment effect with t-tests and OLS, with and without controls, and explored heterogeneous effects by fandom, employment and hometown.
Result
The rivalry message showed a positive but not statistically significant effect (p = 0.15), with statistical power of only about 0.30. My takeaway: the next version needs a power analysis up front, which points to roughly 240 participants to detect an effect of this size.
